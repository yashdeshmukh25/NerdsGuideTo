Coffee 101: from cherry to cup.

Everything a curious beginner needs to actually understand coffee — the beans, the roast, the grind, the brew method, and the whole family tree of espresso drinks. Explained by a nerd, to a caveman, so nobody feels dumb.

Chapter 1

Wait, what even is coffee?

Coffee is a brewed drink made from roasted coffee seeds — people call them "beans," but they're actually the pit inside a small fruit called a coffee cherry.
The Nerd
So we dry out a seed, burn it a little, smash it to bits, and pour hot water through it. On purpose.

Chapter 2

Meet the four species

Every bag of coffee traces back to one of these four plant species — and almost everything you drink is one of the first two.

~60–70% of world production

Arabica

Grown at high altitude in cooler climates. Smoother, sweeter, and more acidic, with delicate floral or fruity notes. Lower caffeine, higher price.

Fun fact: Nearly every "specialty" or single-origin bag you see is Arabica.

~30–40% of world production

Robusta

Grown at lower altitudes on a hardier, disease-resistant plant. Bolder, harsher, more bitter — with roughly double the caffeine.

Fun fact: It also produces more crema, so it sneaks into a lot of espresso blends.

<2% of world production

Liberica

Rare, large, irregularly shaped beans mostly grown in the Philippines and Malaysia. Woody, smoky, almost floral-fruity flavor unlike anything else.

Fun fact: It's called "Barako" coffee in the Philippines and has a cult following.

A rare Liberica relative

Excelsa

Once classified as its own species, now considered a Liberica variety. Tart, fruity, and a little dark-roast-y — often used to add complexity to blends.

Fun fact: It brings both tart and dark notes to the same cup — chaotic, but it works.

Chapter 3

From cherry to cup

Every bean goes on the same journey before it ever meets water.

Grow & Ripen

Coffee cherries ripen on the plant over 7–9 months, usually near the equator.

Harvest

Ripe cherries are hand- or machine-picked, ideally at peak red ripeness.

Process

The fruit is removed from the seed — washed, natural, or honey method.

Dry & Mill

Beans are dried to ~11% moisture, then milled to remove any remaining husk.

Roast

Green beans are roasted at high heat, turning brown and developing flavor.

Grind & Brew

Roasted beans are ground to the right size and brewed into your cup.

Washed

Fruit is stripped off before drying. Clean, bright, and acidic — the flavor is all bean, no fruit.

Natural

Cherries dry whole, fruit still on. Heavy body, boozy-sweet, fruit-forward flavor.

Honey

Some sticky fruit ("mucilage") stays on during drying. Sweet, syrupy, right in between.

Chapter 4

Roast levels, decoded

Same bean, wildly different flavor — it all depends on how long and how hot it's roasted.

Light

Bright, acidic, origin flavor front and center. No surface oil.

Medium

Balanced sweetness and acidity. The everyday crowd-pleaser.

Medium-Dark

Fuller body, deeper flavor, a hint of surface sheen.

Dark

Bold, smoky, bittersweet. Oily surface, less origin character.

French

Almost black, very oily. Charred and smoky — roast flavor wins.

Chapter 6

Pick your brewing method

Same beans, completely different cup — every method extracts coffee a little differently.

Espresso

Fine grind~25–30 sec

Hot water is forced through tightly packed grounds at high pressure. Concentrated, syrupy, and the base for every milk drink below.

Pour-Over

Medium-fine~3 min

Water is poured slowly and manually over grounds in a filter cone (like a V60). Clean, bright, and highlights delicate flavors.

French Press

Coarse~4 min

Grounds steep directly in hot water, then get pressed out with a metal mesh plunger. Full body, no paper filter to soak up the oils.

AeroPress

Medium-fine~1–2 min

Part immersion, part pressure — grounds steep briefly, then get pushed through a paper filter. Fast, forgiving, and travel-friendly.

Moka Pot

Fine-medium~5 min

Stovetop pressure pushes boiling water up through grounds. Strong, bold, and about as close to espresso as most kitchens get.

Cold Brew

Extra coarse12–24 hrs

Grounds steep in cold or room-temp water for a very long time. Smooth, low-acid, naturally sweet — usually served over ice.

Chapter 8

Nerd vocabulary, defined

Crema
The reddish-brown foam on top of a fresh espresso shot, from trapped CO₂ and oils.
Extraction
How much flavor water pulls from the grounds — "under" tastes sour, "over" tastes bitter.
Body
How heavy or thick a coffee feels on your tongue, from watery to syrupy.
Acidity
The bright, tangy quality in coffee — not sourness, more like the zing in citrus.
Single-Origin
Beans from one specific farm, region, or country, rather than a blend.
Terroir
How soil, altitude, and climate shape a bean's flavor — borrowed straight from wine.
Cupping
The formal tasting process pros use to score and compare coffees, like a wine tasting.
Green Coffee
Unroasted coffee seeds — pale green, grassy-smelling, and shelf-stable for years.
